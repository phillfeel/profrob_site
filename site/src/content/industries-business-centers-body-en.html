02 — PROBLEMS

Where office cleaning loses money.

  1. Elevator lobbies and lobby

    Cleaning happens with tenants around.

    Common areas are busy all day. Cleaning runs during working hours and mustn’t disturb people: quiet, no puddles, no blocked walkways.

  2. Building-wide

    Cleaning relies on scarce workers.

    Over 80% of cleaning staff are foreign nationals, cleaning companies’ payroll has grown two- to threefold in five years, and demand for cleaning workers rose 31% in a year.

    CORE.XP and DAKO Professional; Avito Rabota, H1 2026

  3. Engineering

    Older buildings aren’t robot-ready.

    There are no water fill and drain points for docking stations, elevators can’t take a robot’s call, and people open doors by hand.

    What a building needs, per developer MR Group’s automated cleaning standard

  4. Facade

    Facades: work at height.

    Glass cleaning is work at height: certifications, a permit-to-work, and no work in exposed spots when wind reaches 15 m/s. Every cleaning means a contractor with rope access technicians.

    Work-at-height safety rules, Russian Ministry of Labour Order No. 782n

02 Roof · facade

Facade and glass cleaning

The K3 robot descends from the roof on a cable and cleans continuous glazing while people stay on the roof. It can’t clear horizontal frames over 10 mm, so we check the facade’s suitability during the assessment.
