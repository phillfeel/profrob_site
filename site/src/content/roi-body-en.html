Our method
  1. We compare it with what you now pay your rope access contractor: glazed area × number of cleanings × price per m².
  2. The robot cleans about 90% of the glazing. Rope access technicians finish corners, canopies and obstructed sections at your price.
  3. The robot needs a crew of two: an operator and a roof safety attendant. We cost their wages, including income tax and social contributions, for every shift.
  4. The season runs from mid-April to mid-October. A quarter of the days are lost to rain and wind, and work happens on weekdays. One robot handles about 147,000 m² a year. If the volume is larger, we include a second robot in the calculation.
  5. Savings = what you pay the contractor now − robot cost, brushes and consumables, service at 8% of the price per year, and manual touch-up.
  6. Payback is how many months of savings it takes to cover the purchase. The 5-year net gain is the savings over the service life minus the price of the robots.
  7. Leasing: 20% down payment, 36 months. We show how much you keep each month after the lease payment.
  8. The robot works on continuous glazing. We check the facade’s suitability and roof mounting space during the assessment.

Our method
  1. The robot only handles flat hard floors, about 60% of the area in an office. Restrooms, desks and trash stay with people.
  2. We calculate how many full-time positions (FTEs) are freed from floor cleaning, with a margin: 30% of the floor work stays with people.
  3. If you have more cleaners than the norm, each one cleans less, so the same robot frees more FTEs. But never more than half the team.
  4. Savings = wages with contributions + chemicals and water − robot service, consumables and a share of our platform operator’s time.
  5. Payback is how many months of savings it takes to cover the purchase. The 5-year net gain is the savings over the service life minus the price of the robots.
  6. Leasing: 20% down payment, 36 months. We show how much you keep each month after the lease payment.
