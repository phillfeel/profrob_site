01 — MUNICIPAL SERVICES

Robots for cleaning parks, streets, waterfronts.

Grounds are large, there’s plenty of work and ever fewer people to do it. Leaf fall, snowfall and poplar fluff season aren’t getting any shorter.

Robots take the long, repetitive passes along park paths, sidewalks and parking lots; people take work that needs hands and eyes. We choose equipment to fit how you buy.

04 — HOW TO BUY

First the procurement law, then the robot model.

Municipal services buy under Russia’s procurement laws, 44-FZ and 223-FZ. How you procure determines which equipment you can consider at all.

National procurement regime

Route A

You buy equipment.

  1. The buyer sets the OKPD2 code from the manufacturer’s documents.
  2. If the robot falls under an item 98 code, a foreign machine can’t be bought.
  3. So: a Russian manufacturer, or Route B.
Manufacturers ↓
Route B

You buy cleaning.

  1. The purchase covers grounds upkeep: areas, regulations, quality.
  2. The contractor owns and services the robots and answers for the result.
  3. Suits pilots and seasonal work: you pay for cleaned grounds, not for a machine.
Discuss cleaning as a service ↓

This is not legal advice. The buyer decides which OKPD2 code (Russian product classification) a robot falls under, based on the manufacturer’s documents. Compact robots may fall under a different code. Check the current decree with a lawyer before buying. Names of Russian documents here are our own translations.

Sidewalks. The experimental legal regime for robots on sidewalks (Government Decree No. 1247 of 30 September 2026) targets delivery robots. Open sources don’t say whether it covers cleaning robots. In parks, the robot works on the customer’s own grounds.
