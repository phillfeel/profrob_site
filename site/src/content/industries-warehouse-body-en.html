01 — WAREHOUSING

Robots for warehouses and logistics.

A warehouse is a flow: pallets arrive, goods are picked into orders, orders leave. Robots take over moving and repetitive operations; people stay where a decision or an oversized load is involved.

It is better to start with one route than with the whole warehouse: the effect shows quickly and the investment stays small.

03 — ORDER PATH

Warehouse order path.

Five stages, two questions: what the robot takes and what stays with people. An example for a typical distribution center; we adapt it to your warehouse during the site assessment.

  1. A

    Receiving, unloading

    Robot

    A robotic arm unloads cartons from the pallet; a pallet-handling robot takes the pallet from the dock.

    People

    Checking against documents, quality control, non-standard loads.

    Learn more
  2. B

    Storage and moving

    Robot

    Pallet-handling robots and AMRs move pallets between the dock, racks and zones.

    People

    Placing oversized goods, decisions on stock.

    Learn more
  3. C

    Order picking

    Robot

    An AMR brings a rack to the worker or follows the picker.

    People

    Take the item from the cell, check the order.

    Learn more
  4. D

    Sorting and packing

    Robot

    Robots sort orders by destination and count stock.

    People

    Packing non-standard orders, returns.

    Learn more
  5. E

    Shipping

    Robot

    A palletizer builds the pallet; a pallet-handling robot takes it to the dock.

    People

    Loading the truck, documents, control.

    Learn more

Safety near people – per the ISO 3691-4 standard: detects a person, slows down, stops.

06 — FIRST STEPS

Start with one route.

  1. 01

    Site assessment

    We look at flows, aisle widths, floor surface, thresholds and docks. We choose the route where a robot pays off soonest.

  2. 02

    One route

    The robot runs one route under your normal load, without stopping the warehouse. Tasks come from your WMS or 1C.

  3. 03

    Review and decision

    We compare distance walked, speed and errors before and after. Then we decide which routes and operations to move to robots next.
