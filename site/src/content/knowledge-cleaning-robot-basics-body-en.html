Cleaning robot: how it works and which areas it suits.

A cleaning robot is an autonomous machine: it builds a map of the premises from lidar and cameras, follows a set route, goes around obstacles on its own and returns to its station. It suits large, open areas with level floors. In tight spaces with steps and cluttered floors it often does not pay off.

Below, in order: how the robot finds its way, what it does on its own, how much floor it covers on one charge and what to check before buying. The figures come from the datasheet of one model, which we name. Other models differ.

What a robot is made of

Four units. Knowing what each one does makes any datasheet easier to read and models easier to compare.

  • Sensors

    Lidar and cameras

    In the Phantas these are a 2D lidar, depth cameras and a color camera, plus anti-drop and anti-collision sensors. The lidar measures the distance to walls and items, the cameras spot obstacles, and the sensors act as a backstop if everything else fails.

  • Mapping

    Map and position

    From the sensor data the software builds a map and keeps track of where the robot is on it (this is called SLAM). Gausium states a positioning accuracy of ±2 cm using lidar.

  • Working unit

    Brushes, blade, tanks

    Some robots clean dry (sweeping, vacuuming), some clean wet (a water tank and a squeegee), and some do both. The Phantas scrubs, vacuums, sweeps and dust-mops. Clean-water tank 11.5 L, waste-water tank 10.5 L, dust bag 6 L.

  • Station

    Charge and water

    The station charges the robot and manages the water. The manufacturer adds that it also cleans the squeegee, the suction pipe and the waste-water tank by itself. Without a station, a person does these steps.

How the robot finds its way

No floor markings are needed. The robot navigates by a map it built itself. In this it resembles a warehouse AMR, not an AGV, which needs tape or marks.

  1. 01

    Map

    The robot drives around the premises and builds a map. Gausium quotes 30 minutes for a 2,000 m² site. Real time depends on the layout and the number of items.

  2. 02

    Zones

    In the app the operator edits the map and picks the cleaning zones. A whole floor does not have to be one task.

  3. 03

    Route

    The robot plans the path itself: a zigzag or a spiral, as in the Phantas modes. It covers a zone the same way every time, so quality does not depend on who is on shift.

  4. 04

    Avoiding obstacles

    While working, the robot checks the map and goes around people and things that are not on it. According to the manufacturer, it avoids cables on the floor but drives over low thresholds.

  5. 05

    Station and alerts

    Task done or battery low: back to the station. If there is debris nearby that it cannot pick up, in spot-cleaning mode it sends the operator a message.

What the robot does, what people do

A robot does not replace cleaning altogether. It takes the level floor and the repeating routes.

The robot does

  • Level floor in large open areas
  • The same route every time
  • Night and off-peak shifts
  • Avoiding people and items
  • Reports and alerts through the platform

People keep

  • Restrooms
  • Spot cleaning: corners, edges, stains
  • Quality control
  • Large debris and spills
  • Stairs and places the robot cannot reach

The right-hand column is an engineering estimate based on the limits of the technology, not manufacturer data. For your site we refine the list during the assessment.

This is our calculation, not a guarantee: output drops with a tight layout and many obstacles. For a larger site, a recharge during the day, a second shift or several robots help. Charging takes about two hours, so plan the schedule in advance.

Where this works in practice: offices and office buildings, shopping malls, warehouses, clinics and hotels. Our payback calculator handles sites from 3,500 m², warehouses from 10,000 m². That is the limit of the calculation, not a physical limit of the robot: on a smaller area there is usually too little work for it.

When a robot does not fit

Five signs of a site where a cleaning robot most often does not pay off. This is our engineering judgement.

  • Little workA small area or one short shift: the robot sits idle and the savings do not cover the investment.
  • CrampedNarrow aisles and frequent turns eat time, and output falls below the datasheet figure.
  • Steps and level changesBetween floors without an elevator the robot has to be carried, and it does not take high thresholds.
  • Cluttered floorBoxes, furniture and pallets in a new place every day: the route breaks and the robot stops.
  • Large debris and heavy soilingPeople clear it by hand.

If your site matches these points, we will say so at the assessment and suggest something else: rental, retrofitting existing equipment, or dropping the project.

What to check before buying

Six questions to answer before signing a contract.

  1. 01

    Area by floor type

    Hard floor and carpet are cleaned by different units; in the datasheet they are different modes with different output.

  2. 02

    Is the path level

    Thresholds, elevators, doors that open on their own. One spot where the robot gets stuck breaks the whole route.

  3. 03

    When to clean

    At night, during a shift or with people around. The number of shifts and the payback period depend on it.

  4. 04

    Station location

    You need a power socket and a place to drain water near the working zone.

  5. 05

    Who maintains it

    Service and spare parts in Russia, response and recovery times. With us they are fixed in the SLA.

  6. 06

    How payback is counted

    Costs with the robot must include service, consumables and operator time, otherwise the period looks attractive and is wrong.

Common questions

Are floor markings needed?

No. The robot builds a map of the premises from lidar and cameras and navigates by it. That is why it can be reconfigured when the layout of a hall changes.

Can the robot run while people are in the room?

Yes, the manufacturer states that it avoids moving obstacles in real time. Night is still more convenient: fewer people in the way and fewer stops.

Will it replace a cleaner?

Not entirely. The robot takes level floor and repeating routes, and staff move to tasks it does not do: restrooms, spot cleaning, quality control. How many people are freed up we calculate at the assessment.
